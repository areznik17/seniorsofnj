Food and utility help for New Jersey seniors: where to start

SNAP, senior meals, farmers market benefits, heating aid and free weatherization can all ease a tight budget. Here is how each works and how to apply this season.

Groceries and heating bills hit fixed incomes hard. New Jersey has a long list of programs that help, and many older adults qualify for more than one. The trick is knowing where to start. Most of these programs share applications, and one phone call can open several doors.

This guide covers food benefits, meals, heating and electric help, free home energy upgrades and water bills. It also shows you what to bring when you apply.

Can I get SNAP as an older adult?

Yes, and the rules are friendlier for people 60 and older. SNAP (the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, once called food stamps) puts money on an EBT card for groceries. In New Jersey, eligible households get at least $95 a month. The federal maximum for a one-person household from October 2026 is $306 a month.

Special rules for households with someone 60 or older

  • No gross income test. A household with a member 60 or older, or with a disability, can qualify even if its income is above the standard limits. Only the net income test applies, after deductions.
  • Medical costs count. Out-of-pocket medical expenses above $35 a month can be deducted. That can include costs such as Medicare premiums, copays and prescriptions.
  • Housing costs count in full. Rent, mortgage, property taxes and utilities can all lower your countable income, with no cap for older households.
  • Longer approvals. Older adults' cases can last 24 months before renewal.
  • You can apply on your own. An older adult living with family may be able to apply as a separate household in some situations.
A note on work rules

A 2025 federal law expanded SNAP work requirements to adults up to age 64 without young dependents. Covered adults must log 80 hours a month of work, training or volunteering unless they qualify for an exemption, such as a disability or medical condition. People 65 and older are exempt. If you are 60 to 64, ask your county agency how the rule applies to you.

Apply through your County Board of Social Services. You can usually complete the process by phone or online, without an in-person visit. Free SNAP Navigators can help you fill out the form. Find one at NJSNAPNavigators.gov, or dial 2-1-1.

Many older adults skip SNAP because they assume they will only get a few dollars. In New Jersey, the minimum is $95 a month, or $1,140 a year. Medical and housing deductions often push the amount higher.

What about farmers market vouchers?

The Senior Farmers Market Nutrition Program gives low-income adults 60 and older money to spend on fresh New Jersey produce, herbs and honey at approved farmers markets and farm stands. Household income must be under 185% of the federal poverty level. In 2026, Bergen and Gloucester counties gave $40 per person, now loaded as an e-benefit or QR code instead of paper vouchers.

The program runs through your county office on aging, usually in early summer, and benefits go first come, first served. This year's season has mostly wrapped up, so call your county in late spring to get on the list for 2027.

Where can I get meals?

Congregate meals

Senior centers, churches and community sites across the state serve hot meals to adults 60 and older and their spouses of any age. There is no fixed price. You contribute what you can afford. Meals are served at least once a day, five or more days a week, and many sites ask you to reserve a day ahead. They are a good way to see friends, too.

Home-delivered meals

If illness, disability or isolation keeps you at home, you may qualify for home-delivered meals, often called Meals on Wheels. Adults 60 and older are eligible after a needs assessment. Programs deliver at least one meal a day, five days a week, and some add weekend meals. The meals are federally funded, with a suggested contribution.

Both meal programs run through your county Area Agency on Aging. Call the Aging and Disability Resource Connection at 1-877-222-3737 to reach yours.

Food banks and senior food boxes

Food banks supply the local pantries in your town. Some also run the Commodity Supplemental Food Program, which delivers monthly food boxes for older adults, mostly at senior housing buildings. It is not offered everywhere, so call to ask about a site near you.

How do I get help with heating and electric bills?

New Jersey bundles three programs into one application. The Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) helps with heating costs. The Universal Service Fund (USF) lowers your monthly gas and electric bills. The Weatherization Assistance Program makes free energy-saving repairs. Check the weatherization box on the form to be considered for all three.

Applications for this season are open from October 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027, and help is first come, first served. Apply early.

  • LIHEAP heating help depends on your income, household size, fuel type and region. It can be paid to your utility or, for oil and propane, by check to you and your supplier.
  • Medically necessary cooling pays a fixed $300 if someone in the home needs air conditioning for health reasons.
  • USF credits help pay gas and electric costs above a set share of your income, up to $200 a month.

Apply online at energyassistance.nj.gov, or call 1-800-510-3102 to find your local agency. Applicants 60 and older, or with a disability, may apply by mail or ask for a home visit.

Other energy programs to know

  • Comfort Partners offers a free home energy visit and upgrades to help lower bills. Income must be at or below 250% of the federal poverty level, about $39,900 a year for one person in 2026. You need an individually metered gas or electric account. Call 1-866-378-4345. If you heat with oil, propane or kerosene, use the state weatherization program instead.
  • NJ SHARES helps households that earn too much for LIHEAP and USF, roughly 200% to 400% of the poverty level, who have fallen behind. Call 1-866-657-4273.
  • PAGE (Payment Assistance for Gas and Electric) helps low and moderate income residents who are at least 45 days behind or have a shutoff notice. Dial 2-1-1 for the current intake agency.
  • Lifeline pays $225 a year toward utilities if you are 65 or older, or on Social Security disability, with income under $54,943 single or $62,390 married. It is part of the NJSave application described in our guide to prescription and bill help.
If the bill is already overdue, call before the shutoff notice, not after.

Is there help with water bills?

New Jersey's Department of Community Affairs has run a Low Income Household Water Assistance Program (LIHWAP) for households that pay their own water and sewer bills and earn at or below 60% of state median income. Whether it is taking applications depends on funding. Call 1-800-510-3102 to ask whether it is open.

Also call your water utility or town water department directly. Many offer payment plans, and some towns run their own assistance. Keep paying what you can each month while you wait for an answer.

How do I apply, and what should I bring?

  1. Collect proof of income. Your Social Security award letter or SSA-1099, pension statements and any wage stubs for everyone in the household.
  2. Gather your bills. Recent gas, electric and water bills, plus your fuel oil delivery receipts if you have them.
  3. Show your housing costs. Lease, mortgage statement or property tax bill.
  4. Save medical receipts. For SNAP, list Medicare premiums, copays, pharmacy costs and medical transportation.
  5. Bring ID. A photo ID and Social Security numbers for household members who are applying.
  6. Apply to everything you may qualify for. Start with the energy application and SNAP. Then call your county office on aging about meals and next year's farmers market benefits.
Example: a widow in Vineland

Say you are 74, live alone, and get $1,900 a month from Social Security. You heat with oil and have steady pharmacy bills. At $22,800 a year, you are well under the $51,273 LIHEAP limit, so you could get heating help and free weatherization. Because you are over 60, SNAP skips the gross income test and counts your medical and housing costs, so you may also qualify for at least $95 a month in food benefits. Your county office on aging can tell you about meal sites near you.

If you get stuck, dial 2-1-1 (or 1-800-510-3102) any time, or call the Aging and Disability Resource Connection at 1-877-222-3737. For more ways to stretch a budget, see our guides to property tax relief and free and discounted things to do in New Jersey.

Common questions

Do I have to visit an office to apply for SNAP?

Usually not. New Jersey lets you complete the SNAP process by phone or online. A free SNAP Navigator can help you with the form.

Can I get LIHEAP if I heat with oil?

Yes. LIHEAP covers oil, propane and other fuels. Payments for delivered fuel are typically made by check to you and your supplier together.

Who can get home-delivered meals?

Adults 60 and older who are homebound because of illness, disability or isolation, after a needs assessment. Call the ADRC at 1-877-222-3737 to reach your county program.

Is there a deadline for energy assistance?

The 2026 to 2027 application period runs from October 1, 2026, to June 30, 2027, but help is first come, first served, so apply early.
