The Jersey Shore after Labor Day: a town-by-town guide

Quiet beaches, easy parking and boardwalks you can actually stroll. Here is what stays open, where beach wheelchairs and mats are, and how to get there without driving.

Ask anyone who lives at the Shore and they will tell you September and October are the best months. The water is still warm, the crowds are gone, and you can park near the boardwalk. Many restaurants and shops stay open on weekends well into fall.

There is one catch for anyone who needs help on sand. Most beach wheelchair programs are run by the beach patrol, and the lifeguards go home after the summer season. We checked each town's official information in October 2026. Below we tell you what we found and who to call.

After Labor Day the boardwalk belongs to the walkers, the dog owners and the people who brought a good book.

Monmouth County: which towns are easiest to reach by train?

NJ Transit's North Jersey Coast Line stops at Asbury Park, Spring Lake and Point Pleasant Beach. Riders 62 and older pay reduced fares. Have proof of age with you.

Ocean Grove

This Victorian camp meeting town is calm in any season, and it is even calmer now. Walk the boardwalk, then stroll past the tent cottages and the Great Auditorium. Check the Ocean Grove Camp Meeting Association calendar for organ concerts and holiday programs.

Accessibility: In summer, beach wheelchairs wait at the Webb Avenue, Embury Avenue, Ocean Pathway and Bath Avenue entrances, and staff push you down to the sand. In 2026 the guarded season ran daily through September 7. Ask the association whether chairs are available after that.

Good to know: Leashed dogs are allowed on the beach and boardwalk from September 15 to May 15.

Asbury Park

The boardwalk here runs past Convention Hall and the Stone Pony. In fall the calendar fills with weekend markets, like record shows and vintage sales. The train station is in town, and NJ Transit bus 317 runs from Philadelphia to the Asbury Park Transportation Center.

Spring Lake

Spring Lake is known for its quiet boardwalk and grand old houses. When the season ends, the borough closes its beach pavilions, pools and bathrooms, so plan your restroom stop in town. Badges are sold online during the season. Questions: Borough of Spring Lake, 732-449-0800.

Point Pleasant Beach

Jenkinson's Aquarium on the boardwalk is open daily, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., except Thanksgiving and Christmas. That makes it a dependable rainy-day stop. Seniors 65 and older pay $15 (adults $19). The aquarium's wheelchair lift is being replaced until about October 31, so call 732-892-0600 first. Point Pleasant Beach has a train station, and NJ Transit bus 830 runs from Asbury Park station, with no Sunday service.

Ocean County and Atlantic City: what is open and accessible?

Long Beach Island

LBI in the fall is all lighthouse views, bay sunsets and empty beaches. Long Beach Township lends beach wheelchairs free to people staying at a township address. Chairs are delivered and picked up on Saturdays and reserved a week at a time. An attendant must push. Call the beach patrol at 609-361-1200 to ask about dates, or see the Beachwheels page. NJ Transit's shore page does not list LBI service, so plan to drive.

Atlantic City

Atlantic City is the most accessible beach town on this list in the off-season, and its beaches are free. Here is what the city's tourism office lists:

  • Beach mats: 175-foot access mats at the Tallahassee, Bartram, Albany and Raleigh Avenue entrances.
  • Ramps: Accessible ramps lead from the Boardwalk to the beach throughout the city.
  • Surf chairs: Lent first come, first served by the Beach Patrol at Mississippi Avenue. Call 609-347-5307 to check.
  • Year-round restrooms: At New York, Mississippi and Chelsea avenues on the Boardwalk.

Details are on the Atlantic City access page.

Getting there by transit: The Atlantic City Rail Line runs daily from Philadelphia's 30th Street Station to the Atlantic City Rail Terminal, a few blocks from the Boardwalk. From North Jersey, NJ Transit bus 319 runs express from Port Authority with stops at Newark Penn Station and Journal Square. Bus 551 runs from Philadelphia. Bus 319 excursion tickets are sold through the NJ Transit mobile app or at the Port Authority Bus Terminal, so buy before you board.

Cape May County: what do the southern towns offer?

Ocean City

Ocean City's boardwalk runs about 2.5 miles. The local chamber says many restaurants, arcades and shops stay open through the off-season, which it counts from November to March. The Music Pier hosts concerts into the fall.

Accessibility: Ocean City runs a free surf chair program from the 46th Street Information Center, 46th Street and West Avenue, open 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Chairs can be borrowed for a day or up to a week. Call 609-554-0352 for information or reservations. For the boardwalk itself, the city lends a few regular wheelchairs free, two hours at a time, from the Music Pier Information Center between 8th and 9th streets. Call 609-399-6111 ext. 9337. Accessible restrooms are at 1st, 6th, 10th, 11th and 12th streets, the Music Pier, the 34th Street playground and the 58th Street beach. Shaded boardwalk pavilions near 8th through 12th streets are a good place to sit. Ask which restrooms stay open after the summer.

The Wildwoods

Wildwood's beach is wide and free. The city says ADA beach wheelchairs are available, plus a beach taxi from four pickup points. The taxi runs Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day weekend, 10 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Accessible paths under the boardwalk are at East Bennett, East Spicer and East Maple avenues. To arrange help, call 609-846-2012. NJ Transit buses 313 and 315 serve Wildwood and Cape May all year.

Cape May

Fall may be Cape May's finest season. Since 2024 the city has laid about two miles of ADA beach mats, running parallel to the ocean from Madison Avenue at the east end of the promenade to the Cove at Third Avenue, with platforms that reach toward the water. The city also keeps 12 beach wheelchairs at entrances along the beachfront, first come, first served, from 10 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. To make special arrangements, call the Cape May Beach Patrol at 609-884-9520. Beach tags are required only from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day. In summer, free public restrooms sit about every half mile along the promenade, so ask which stay open in fall. Dogs are allowed on Cape May city beaches from November through March, so expect some furry company.

Cape May MAC runs fall and holiday programs, including Victorian Weekend, Halloween Happenings and Christmas in Cape May. Its Historic District trolley tour runs all year, and one trolley has a wheelchair lift. Reserve it two weeks ahead at 609-884-5404. The lighthouse has 199 steps and no elevator, but the oil house exhibit at its base is accessible.

Should I drive or take the train?

Driving gives you the most freedom, and fall parking is far easier than in July. Parking rules differ from town to town, and some meters run all year, so read the signs before you walk away. If you have a disability placard, bring it. Accessible spaces near boardwalk ramps fill first on sunny weekends.

The train is a relaxing choice for Monmouth County towns. The North Jersey Coast Line reaches Asbury Park, Spring Lake and Point Pleasant Beach. Ask how far the station is from where you want to go, and line up a taxi or ride if the walk is long. For Atlantic City, the train and the 319 bus both end near the Boardwalk. Check schedules on the NJ Transit Jersey Shore page before you leave, since fall timetables are thinner than summer ones.

What happens at the Shore every fall?

  • Hawk migration at Cape May Point. New Jersey Audubon counts hawks from a platform beside the state park lot every day from September 1 to November 30. Many of the best flights come in late September and October.
  • The Cape May Fall Festival. New Jersey Audubon's birding festival is held each October, with events at Cape May Convention Hall. In 2026 it ran October 15 to 18. Its Northwood Center also runs relaxed "E-Z Birding" walks.
  • Halloween and holiday programs. Cape May MAC's Halloween and Christmas events return each year. Ocean City's long-running Halloween Parade travels Asbury Avenue downtown, on October 29 in 2026 at 7:15 p.m.
  • Weekend markets and races. Asbury Park's boardwalk hosts markets. The Atlantic City Marathon weekend is held in mid-October (October 17 and 18 in 2026), so expect road closures and busy hotels that weekend.

Check each organizer's website for this year's dates before you go.

How do I plan an easy off-season Shore day?

  1. Pick one town. Shore traffic is light in fall, but the Garden State Parkway still adds up. One town and one meal makes a good day.
  2. Call about access the week before. Ask whether beach wheelchairs or mats are still out, and which restrooms are open.
  3. Check the weather and the wind. A sunny 60-degree day on the boardwalk can feel much colder by the water. Bring a windbreaker and a hat.
  4. Go midweek if you can. Some businesses cut back to weekends in the fall, so check restaurant hours first. The upside is easy parking.
  5. Use your senior fare. If you take NJ Transit, show proof of age for the reduced fare. Your free State Parks Senior Citizen Pass covers park entrance, including Cape May Point and Island Beach.
Common mistake

Assuming summer services still run. Lifeguards, beach taxis and many surf chair programs stop after Labor Day. One phone call saves a long walk on soft sand.

What if sand is too hard for me?

You can still have a wonderful day. Sit on a boardwalk bench and watch the surf. Ride a Cape May trolley with a lift. Visit the Jenkinson's aquarium once its lift is back in service. In Atlantic City, roll down a ramp and out along a beach mat. A boardwalk lunch with a view counts as a Shore day too.

For more outings, see our list of easy day trips in New Jersey and our guide to free and discounted things for NJ seniors. If you are going with grandchildren, see our guide to days out with the grandkids.

Common questions

Are New Jersey beaches free after Labor Day?

Many towns only require beach tags during the summer guarded season. Cape May, for example, requires them from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day. Atlantic City and Wildwood beaches are free all year.

Can I get a beach wheelchair at the Shore in the fall?

Possibly, but call first. Most programs are run by the beach patrol in summer. Atlantic City lends surf chairs at Mississippi Avenue (609-347-5307), and Cape May's Beach Patrol lends free chairs (609-884-9520).

Which Shore town has beach mats for wheelchairs?

Atlantic City has 175-foot access mats at the Tallahassee, Bartram, Albany and Raleigh Avenue entrances, plus ramps from the Boardwalk.

Can I get to the Shore without a car?

Yes. The North Jersey Coast Line serves Asbury Park, Spring Lake and Point Pleasant Beach. The Atlantic City Rail Line and bus 319 reach Atlantic City. Buses 313 and 315 serve Wildwood and Cape May all year. Riders 62 and older pay reduced fares.

Does Ocean City lend beach wheelchairs?

Yes. Ocean City's free surf chair program operates from the 46th Street Information Center, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., for a day or up to a week. Call 609-554-0352. A few regular wheelchairs for the boardwalk can be borrowed at the Music Pier Information Center.

Are dogs allowed on New Jersey beaches in the fall?

In some towns. Ocean Grove allows leashed dogs on the beach and boardwalk from September 15 to May 15, and Cape May city beaches allow dogs from November through March. Rules differ by town, so check before you go.
